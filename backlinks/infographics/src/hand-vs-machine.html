The 30 second test

Hand or machine? Turn the garment inside out.

The front of good machine embroidery can look very close to handwork. The back almost never does. This is what each one usually looks like from the inside.

Hand embroidery, back side

Made with a needle, one stitch at a time

  • 1Small knots and short thread tails where each colour starts and stops
  • 2Long "travel" threads jumping from one motif to the next
  • 3Stitch lengths and angles vary slightly, even in a neat piece
  • 4Usually no stiff backing patch behind the design

Machine embroidery, back side

Sewn by a multi-needle machine from a digital file

  • 1A white bobbin thread line down the middle of satin stitches
  • 2A stabiliser patch (paper or mesh) cut around the design
  • 3Perfectly even, parallel stitches that repeat exactly
  • 4Tiny trimmed thread stubs instead of knots

Five checks before you pay a handmade price

  1. Turn it inside out and look for knots and tails.
  2. Look for a white bobbin line inside satin areas.
  3. Feel for a stiff stabiliser patch behind the design.
  4. Compare two repeated motifs. Identical twins mean machine.
  5. Ask the seller how many hours the piece took. Makers know.
