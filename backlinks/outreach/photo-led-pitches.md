# Pitches that need your photos first

These three publications decide mostly on pictures. A pitch without good photos will be turned down, so these drafts wait until you have the photos below. Everything said about Vee Threads here is already on your homepage or in this repo.

## Photos to take (one session covers all three)

Daylight, no filters, plain background, phone camera is fine if it is sharp.

1. An artisan's hands mid-stitch on a hoop, close enough to see the thread.
2. The same piece, front and back side by side (the back proves it is hand work).
3. A finished name tee or sweatshirt, flat.
4. A detail of fine floral work (Sunita dadi's speciality).
5. A detail of heritage motifs (Rukhsana's speciality).
6. A wider shot of the studio or of artisans working, **only with their permission**, and only those who are happy to be named.

Save full-size originals. For first emails, send small copies (under 1 MB each, about 1200px wide).

## 1. Selvedge (online stories)

- **Why:** a leading textile magazine. Its online Journal runs India features, including one on contemporary chikankari, and invites outside contributors through "Write for Online Stories". Online stories can link to the maker's site.
- **To:** editor@selvedge.org
- **Attach:** photos 1 to 6 (small copies)
- **Subject:** Online story pitch: women hand-embroidering streetwear in Bulandshahr

> Dear Selvedge editors,
>
> I'm Mansi Chaudhary, founder of Vee Threads, a hand embroidery studio in Bulandshahr, Uttar Pradesh. I would like to propose a short online story about five of the women who stitch for us, and what happens when traditional hand embroidery moves onto tees, sweatshirts and caps.
>
> Between them they have more than 90 years of experience. Sunita dadi has done fine floral work for over 25 years. Rukhsana learned from her grandmother and specialises in heritage motifs. Lakshmi chachi, Fatima and Anjali work on names, beadwork and minimal modern designs. Every piece is made to order, and a single oversized tee with a parakeet and floral panel takes 24 hours of stitching.
>
> The story would cover how each of them learned, what changes when the ground is a knitted cotton tee rather than a woven fabric, and why the backs of their pieces look the way they do. I have attached six photographs, and can send full-resolution files.
>
> Some context on the sector, with our own infographics: https://www.veethreads.com/blogs/news/hand-embroidery-india-numbers
>
> Warm regards,
> Mansi Chaudhary
> Founder, Vee Threads
> https://www.veethreads.com/ | Instagram: @vee.threads

**Before sending:** check with the artisans that they are happy to be named and photographed, and fill in one line on what changes when stitching on knit fabric if you want it in the pitch.

## 2. Embroidery magazine (Embroiderers' Guild, UK): maker profile

- **Why:** the specialist magazine for contemporary embroidery. Their guidelines say makers who want a profile should send the editor a 200-word statement, up to six low-resolution images and their website. They aim to reply within 10 days. It is mainly a print magazine, so treat it as credibility first and a link second.
- **To:** johalleditor@gmail.com
- **Attach:** photos 1 to 5 plus one more finished piece (six maximum, low resolution)
- **Subject:** Profile submission: Vee Threads, hand embroidery studio, Bulandshahr, India
- **Rule:** they want work that has not been offered elsewhere at the same time, so do not send this same statement to Selvedge in the same week. The Selvedge pitch above is a different, longer story.

> Dear Editor,
>
> Please consider Vee Threads for a maker profile. Our 200-word statement and six low-resolution images are below and attached, and our website is https://www.veethreads.com/.
>
> **Statement (200 words)**
>
> Vee Threads is a hand embroidery studio in Bulandshahr, Uttar Pradesh, in north India. Every piece we make is stitched by hand by women artisans. Five of them, whom you can meet on our website, have more than ninety years of experience between them: Sunita dadi in fine florals, Rukhsana in heritage motifs she learned from her grandmother, Lakshmi chachi in lettering, Fatima in beadwork and Anjali in minimal modern designs.
>
> We put their work on everyday clothes: tees, sweatshirts, hoodies, caps and socks. Most pieces are personal. A customer sends a name, a date or a small drawing, and one artisan stitches it after the order arrives. Nothing is made for stock.
>
> Hand embroidery on knitted cotton is slow. A single initial takes hours, and our parakeet and floral tee takes twenty-four hours of stitching. A commercial machine sews a five-thousand-stitch logo in about five minutes. We do not try to compete with that. The back of a hand-embroidered piece, with its knots and small irregularities, is the proof that a person made it.
>
> Our aim: steady, properly valued work for skilled women who have often had irregular hours and unpredictable pay, and clothes that people keep.
>
> Warm regards,
> Mansi Chaudhary
> Founder, Vee Threads
> Instagram: @vee.threads

**Before sending:** count the words again if you edit it (it is just under 200 now), and confirm the five artisans are happy to be named.

## 3. PieceWork, Fall 2027 "Craft, Continued" (later, and only if it fits)

- **Why:** a call for profiles of artisans and collectives sustaining traditional handicrafts, with a preference for traditions that get little coverage. Call page: https://pieceworkmagazine.com/craft-continued-piecework-fall-2027-call-for-submissions/
- **Fit check:** this works only if you can say which traditional stitches or regional styles the artisans use (Rukhsana's heritage motifs are the obvious starting point) and how they learned them. A story about streetwear alone will not fit.
- **What to send:** a one-paragraph proposal through the method on the call page, with photos 1, 2 and 5. The deadline is on the call page; check it before writing.
