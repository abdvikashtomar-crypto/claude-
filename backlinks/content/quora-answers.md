## 1. Why is hand embroidery the most expensive?
https://www.quora.com/Why-is-hand-embroidery-the-most-expensive

Because almost all of the price is hours, and hours by hand are long.

A commercial embroidery machine runs at roughly 800 to 1,200 stitches a minute. At 1,000 a minute, a 5,000 stitch logo is done in about five minutes. A person stitching by hand might take most of a working day on a design that size, depending on the stitches.

Some real numbers:

- At our studio, a parakeet and floral design on an oversized tee takes 24 hours of stitching.
- A maker on Reddit logged 25 hours for the front of a sashiko portrait tee and 50 hours for front and back.
- Another used 150 metres of floss over about 19 days of 2 to 4 hour sessions on one shirt.

Thread and fabric are a small part of the cost. If a hand-embroidered piece sells for Rs 2,000, most of that is paying for a skilled person's time, plus the brand's costs for photos, packaging, shipping and returns.

That is also why very cheap "hand embroidered" listings deserve a second look. Turn the garment inside out: hand embroidery shows knots and loose tails on the back; machine embroidery shows a neat stripe of white bobbin thread in the middle of satin stitches.

## 2. How can I identify an authentic hand-embroidered shirt?
https://www.quora.com/How-can-I-identify-an-authentic-hand-embroidered-shirt

Turn it inside out. That settles it most of the time.

**Signs of hand embroidery on the back**
- Small knots or short thread tails where each colour starts and stops.
- Long "travel" threads running from one motif to the next.
- Stitch lengths and angles that vary slightly, even in neat work.

**Signs of machine embroidery on the back**
- A stripe of white or pale bobbin thread down the middle of satin areas (letters, leaves, petals).
- A stiff stabiliser patch, often paper-like or mesh, trimmed around the design.
- Tiny trimmed thread stubs instead of knots.

Two more checks from the front: compare any repeated motif (a machine makes identical twins; hands never do), and look along an outline (machine stitches are perfectly even, like a ruler line).

Then ask the seller how many hours it took. Makers know the number because the time is the cost.

No single sign is proof. Some hand embroiderers finish the back very neatly, and some machine work uses a backing that washes out. Three or more signs pointing the same way is a reliable answer.

## 3. How can you tell if an embroidered blouse on a sari is hand-embroidered or machine-made?
https://www.quora.com/How-can-you-tell-if-an-embroidered-blouse-on-a-sari-is-hand-embroidered-or-machine-made

Look at the inside of the blouse panel, under the lining if you can.

- **Aari or zardozi by hand:** you will see the chain stitch loops or the couching threads on the back, slightly irregular, with knots where the artisan finished a section. Beads and sequins are held by individual stitches you can follow.
- **Machine embroidery:** the back is very regular, often with a white bobbin thread showing through dense areas and a stiff interfacing patch behind the design. Sequins on machine work are often applied in long continuous runs.

On the front, compare the two sleeves or two matching motifs on the yoke. A machine reproduces them exactly. By hand, one flower will always be a little different from its twin.

Price is a clue too. Dense hand aari work on a blouse is days of work, so a very low price for "pure handwork" is unlikely.

*Disclosure: I work in hand embroidery (Vee Threads, Bulandshahr), mostly on tees and sweatshirts rather than saris, but the same checks apply.*

## 4. Does embroidery on shirts become undone in the wash?
https://www.quora.com/Does-embroidery-on-shirts-become-undone-in-the-wash

Good embroidery does not come undone in normal washing. It does wear faster if it meets heat, friction and twisting, which is what most washing machines do.

Hand embroidery is anchored through the fabric, so it cannot peel the way a print or a heat transfer can. The risks are different: a loose loop snagging, colours fading, or the fabric under the stitches shrinking and puckering the design.

What keeps it looking new:

1. Wash inside out.
2. Cold or cool water, 30°C or below.
3. Hand wash, or a delicate cycle inside a mesh laundry bag.
4. Mild detergent, no bleach.
5. Do not wring. Press water out with a towel and dry flat in the shade.
6. Iron from the back with the embroidery face down on a towel.

If a loop does snag, push it back through to the inside with a needle. Do not cut it, because that is how a whole line of stitching starts to open.

## 5. How can an embroidered cloth be washed and dried without damaging the stitches?
https://www.quora.com/How-can-an-embroidered-cloth-be-washed-and-dried-without-damaging-the-stitches

The drying is where most damage happens, so start there.

**Drying**
- Never wring or twist it. Roll it in a dry towel and press.
- Dry flat in the shade. Hanging a heavy wet sweatshirt by the shoulders stretches the fabric around the embroidery.
- Skip the tumble dryer. Heat shrinks cotton fabric slightly while the thread stays the same, which puckers the design.

**Washing**
- Inside out, cold water, mild detergent, no bleach.
- Hand wash if you can. If not, use a mesh bag and the delicate cycle.
- Do not soak it overnight with dark or bright clothes; embroidery threads can pick up dye.

**Ironing**
- Embroidery face down on a folded towel, iron the reverse on medium heat. The towel lets the stitches sink in instead of being flattened.

## 6. Where can I get custom embroidered T-shirts?
https://www.quora.com/Where-can-I-get-custom-embroidered-T-shirts

It depends on whether you want machine or hand embroidery, and how many.

**For logos and bulk orders (20 or more pieces):** a local machine embroidery shop or an online printing marketplace is cheaper and faster. Machine embroidery is consistent and durable, which is what you want for uniforms and team wear.

**For one piece, a gift, a name or a date:** hand embroidery is worth the extra cost. You get texture you can feel and a piece that is genuinely one of a kind. Expect a lead time of a week or more, because it is made after you order.

Questions to ask any seller:
- Is it hand or machine? (Check by turning it inside out when it arrives.)
- What thread do you use, and does it bleed?
- How long does it take, and when will it ship?
- Can I see a photo before it ships?

## 7. What are some good Indian websites to make custom t-shirts, hoodies, sweatshirts (single order and not bulk order)?
https://www.quora.com/What-are-some-good-Indian-websites-to-make-custom-t-shirts-hoodies-sweatshirts-single-order-and-not-bulk-order

Split it by method, because the right site depends on that.

**Printed (cheapest, fastest):** print-on-demand sites and custom tee marketplaces will do a single printed tee or hoodie. Good for photos and full-colour artwork.

**Machine embroidered:** many online stores now do single-piece machine embroidery for names and small logos. Clean, durable, usually a few days.

**Hand embroidered:** slower and pricier, but it is a different product. Each piece is stitched by a person, so it has texture and small variations a machine cannot copy. Good for gifts, names, dates and anything you want to keep for years.

Whichever you pick, check the fabric weight (GSM). A 180 GSM tee is light; 220 to 240 GSM holds embroidery better without puckering. For sweatshirts, look for 300 GSM fleece or heavier.

## 8. Can you hand embroider without a stabilizer?
https://www.quora.com/Can-you-hand-embroidery-without-a-stabilizer

On woven cotton or linen, yes. On a t-shirt or anything stretchy, you really should not.

Knit fabric stretches as you pull each stitch, so lines come out wavy and the design puckers when you take the hoop off. One embroiderer on Reddit put it well: "tshirts stretch like crazy and you'll end up with wavy lines."

What works on tees:
- A soft fusible or wash-away stabiliser on the back. Avoid stiff tear-away types on tees; they leave a scratchy patch inside.
- A hoop with grip (wrap the inner ring with cotton tape) so the fabric does not slide.
- Pre-wash the tee before stitching so any shrinkage happens first.
- Do not pull stitches tight.
- For big designs, work in sections and move the hoop.

For colour planning, a free tool can help: we built a Chrome extension, Thread Color Finder, that matches any colour on screen or in a photo to the nearest DMC floss numbers.

## 9. How do I find personalized gifts online in India?
https://www.quora.com/How-do-I-find-personalized-gifts-online-in-India

A few things make a personalised gift feel personal rather than generic:

1. **Pick something they will use often.** A name on a mug gets used; a name on a showpiece gets dusted. Clothes, caps and bags are worn and seen.
2. **Personalise with something only you know.** A nickname, a date, a pet, a line from a song you both like. The more specific, the better.
3. **Check the method.** Laser engraving and embroidery last. Cheap vinyl prints crack after a few washes.
4. **Order early.** Handmade personalised gifts are made after you order. Leave at least two weeks before festivals like Diwali or Rakhi, when every studio is busy.
5. **Ask for a preview photo** before it ships.

For clothing gifts, hand embroidery is the one that feels most personal. On Reddit, two of the most upvoted hand-embroidered tee posts we found were gifts: one made by students for their physics teacher, and one made for a husband.
